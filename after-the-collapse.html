Parallel Vision / After the Collapse

The city that remains

After the Third World War, Berlin survives in fragments. Trains still cross the surface. Maintenance machines return to damaged streets. Artificial light falls across buildings whose inhabitants have disappeared beneath the city. The surviving infrastructure continues to follow the routines of the old world, even where their purpose has been forgotten.

Below ground, old U-Bahn tunnels have become settlements. Shelters, water systems, medical rooms and sleeping quarters occupy the spaces between former stations. Life gathers around whatever still functions. Access to clean air and protected space determines the distance between safety and exposure. On the surface, masks and protective clothing have become part of the body’s daily negotiation with a damaged environment.

The remains of human culture are kept alongside the means of survival. Photographs, recordings, mechanical objects and worn materials carry histories that the city’s automated systems cannot interpret. Some communities preserve them as a way to recognise what has been lost. Others want the past erased, fearing that its return would restore the hierarchies and violence that brought the world to collapse. Memory has become something people protect, conceal and fight over.

Beneath the lonely streets, people still gather to dance. Clubs shelter conversations that remain unrecorded, private rituals and bodies exhausted by vigilance. Music passes between people without requiring an explanation. Touch restores a sense of physical presence. In these rooms, desire continues beyond the demands of survival: people seek one another without knowing what their closeness will preserve, or what kind of future it might make possible.
